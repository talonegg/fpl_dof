## What four seasons settle

**Ranking and selection are inverted.** The season mean is the *worst* ranker in the field and the *best* selector. Every other model orders the whole field better than it — the component model beats it on rank correlation in 129 of 131 gameweeks — and every other model picks a worse top fifteen.

The likely mechanism: rank correlation is dominated by the mass of players who score nothing, and getting those in the right order is easy for any model that tracks minutes. The top fifteen is a question about the extreme tail, where the responsive models chase recent hauls that do not repeat. Being right about the many does not help you pick the few.

**This overturns the single-season result.** On 2025-26 alone the component model was *indistinguishable* from the benchmark on selection (t = 0.03). Across four seasons it is *significantly worse* (t = -2.56). The extra data did not sharpen a small edge — it revealed the sign was wrong.

**Nothing built in Phase 3 beats a season average at picking players.** The closest is OpponentAdjusted, which is merely indistinguishable. Treat rank correlation as a diagnostic only: optimising it has, so far, made selection worse.

**Caveat.** Four seasons of one league, sharing many of the same players and scoring rules. Independent they are not.
