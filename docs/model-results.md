## Metrics

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `top_15_mean_actual` | **The one that matters.** Points actually scored by the predicted top 15, averaged per gameweek. |
| `field_mean_actual` | What every player averaged. A model must beat this to be worth anything. |
| `top_15_precision` | Share of the predicted top 15 that were in the actual top 15. |
| `rank_correlation` | Spearman correlation of predicted and actual points. |
| `mae` / `rmse` | Error size, in points. Diagnostic only — FPL is a ranking problem. |

## Reading these results

- **Better ranking does not mean better picks.** The shortest form window has the best `rank_correlation` but the worst `top_15_mean_actual`. Ordering the whole field well and choosing the best fifteen are different skills, and only the second one buys players.
- **`Zero` is not a valid floor on the selection metrics.** With every prediction tied, `nlargest` returns whichever rows come first, which is not a random fifteen. Judge `Zero` on `rank_correlation` (undefined, correctly) and ignore its `top_15` figures.
- **`field_mean_actual` is low** because it includes every player in the dataset, most of whom did not play. It is the right comparison for "is this model doing anything", not a realistic alternative strategy.
- **One season is not proof.** These numbers come from a single season of 33 scored gameweeks. Treat a small gap between models as noise.
